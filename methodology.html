Methodology, Scoring Formula & Limitations

Complete mathematical and architectural transparency behind the PathWise decision-support system.

1. The Explainable Recommendation Formula

Unlike black-box machine learning approaches that cannot explain why a recommendation was made, PathWise implements a multi-criteria weighted utility model. Each career's final compatibility score is calculated as a normalized linear combination:

Final Score = (w_interests × S_interests) + (w_skills × S_skills) + (w_tools × S_tools) + (w_education × S_education) + (w_preferences × S_preferences)

Subject to the constraint that all active weights are dynamically normalized to sum to unity:

∑ w_i = 1.0 (100%)
Factor Default Weight (w_i) Computation Logic
Technical Interests 30% (0.30) Set intersection of student-selected topics with the career's O*NET interest tags, normalized by the primary domain depth.
Reported Skills 30% (0.30) Mapping of student ratings (0–5) against the career's top 8 essential skills. (Rating / 5.0) yields an individual sub-score.
Known Tools 15% (0.15) Proportion of industry-standard tools (e.g. Git, VS Code, SQL, Docker) recognized and used by the student.
Education Compatibility 15% (0.15) Evaluation of entry-level academic requirements against BCA / undergraduate Computer Applications curricula.
Work Preferences 10% (0.10) Match on coding intensity (High, Moderate, Low), technical style (Analytical vs Creative), and collaboration mode.
Configurable Choice Note: These weights represent proposed design choices tailored for early-stage BCA career exploration, not scientifically immutable constants. Students can adjust weights dynamically via the Priority Sliders in the recommendations and simulator modules.

2. Handling Missing Data Without Unfair Bias

A common failure mode in decision systems is assigning zero scores when a student omits non-mandatory fields, or inversely giving full credit for unverified claims.

  • Zero Credit for Unreported Skills: If a student has not learned a skill (Level 0), it is flagged as a learning gap rather than generating synthetic credit.
  • Neutral Baseline for Omitted Preferences: If a student leaves work preference fields blank, the system applies a neutral default (0.50) without penalizing their existing academic skills.
  • Weight Normalization: Adding optional factors (like salary or job outlook) automatically scales existing weights proportionally so the total score remains strictly bound between 0% and 100%.

3. Career Readiness Meter Criteria

PathWise rejects misleading labels such as "job ready" for undergraduate students. Instead, we use a transparent preparation scale:

Score ≥ 72%
Better Prepared for Exploration

Strong existing alignment in foundational syntax, known tools, and coursework. Ready to start building capstone projects.

Score 48% – 71%
Developing

Demonstrates emerging aptitude but requires 2–3 targeted skills (e.g. SQL, Git, frameworks) to attain project proficiency.

Score < 48%
Foundation to Build

Early-stage interest where the student should first complete introductory coursework before undertaking advanced tasks.

4. Verified Data Sources & Attribution

  • O*NET Online (v28.0): Occupational skills, importance ratings, and tool classifications are extracted directly from the U.S. Department of Labor's O*NET database (SOC codes 15-xxxx series).
  • U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS): Median annual wage ($) and 10-year occupational projection percentages (2025–2035 National Employment Matrix Table 1.2) provide verified international labor indicators.
  • India Tech Market Salary Benchmark: To prevent misleading students by displaying only high-denomination USD salaries, PathWise provides a separate, explicitly attributed India Tech Industry index in INR Lakhs Per Annum (LPA) reflecting typical entry-to-mid career compensation in major Indian tech hubs.

5. Ethical Limitations & Disclaimers

Mandatory Academic Notice: PathWise is designed exclusively as an educational decision-support explorer for university students.
  • No Job Guarantee: Recommendation scores do not represent a probability of being hired or succeeding in any career.
  • Self-Reporting Limitation: Skill ratings represent student self-perception; they do not replace formal university assessments or comprehensive technical interviews.
  • No Salary Guarantees: Displayed salary benchmarks represent market medians and reference ranges; individual student offers depend on skills, company tier, location, and economic conditions.
  • Metacognitive Calibration: Practical Trial Lab results represent introductory educational tasks, not professional competency examinations.
